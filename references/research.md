# Research origins, cases and limitations (verified November 2026)

## Acknowledge selection bias

- Bessembinder, Chen, Choi & Wei (2023), *Long-Term Shareholder Returns: Evidence from 64,000 Global Stocks*: top 2.4% account for all USD 75.7tn net global wealth creation during 1990-2020. This reflects concentration of realized wealth creation, not a predictive screen, not '2.4% became 10-baggers'. Source: https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2023/long-term-shareholder-returns-evidence-from-64000-global-stocks
- Bessembinder (2018), *Do Stocks Outperform Treasury Bills?*: top US 4% account for lifetime dollar net US stock-market wealth creation since 1926; no suggestion a present-day metric can effortlessly identify them. Source: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304405X18301521

## Established investing frameworks

- Chris Mayer, *100 Baggers*, CFA Society India interview (2021): capital returns and repeated reinvestment at attractive rates. https://cfasocietyindia.org/blog/iic-2021-a-conversation-with-chris-mayer-author-of-100-baggers/
- Motilal Oswal 19th Annual Wealth Creation Study (2009-14): 'SQGLP': size, quality, growth, longevity and price; winner-oriented retrospective framework, not prospective validation. https://ftp.motilaloswal.com/emailer/Research/WC19-20141212-MOSL-2009-14-PG048.pdf
- Michael Mauboussin, capital allocation principles and incremental ROIC: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jacf.12090

## Historical business examples: study only with as-of filings

1. **Monster Beverage** (NASDAQ: MNST). Growth runway + consumer brand + distribution: its 2015 strategic partnership with Coca-Cola expanded distribution opportunities. The 2015 release documents the arrangement; do not backdate the benefits to earlier years. https://investors.monsterbevcorp.com/news-releases/news-release-details/coca-cola-company-and-monster-beverage-corporation-close
2. **Amazon** (NASDAQ: AMZN). In the 1997 shareholder letter, Amazon emphasized cash flow and long-term leadership rather than maximizing near-term reported profitability: https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/company-news/amazons-original-1997-letter-to-shareholders . A naïve current-P/E screen would miss the capital-reinvestment logic.
3. **Constellation Software** (TSX: CSU). Repeated acquisition capital deployment, owner/operator autonomy, ROIC and acquisition economics. Its 2011/2013 President's Letters discuss invested capital, acquisition deployment, and organic growth. https://www.csisoftware.com/investor-relations/
4. **Nvidia** (NASDAQ: NVDA). Platform/ecosystem and expanding compute markets are useful research themes; do not assume current valuation or historical winner status makes future 3x easy. Check dated company 10-Ks and competition around a selected entry date.
5. **Netflix** (NASDAQ: NFLX). Subscriber economics, content costs, distribution scale, competition and operating leverage; test FCF after content spending, not just reported EPS, at the selected entry date.
6. **Copart** (NASDAQ: CPRT). Network density, buyer liquidity, salvage marketplace, regulatory relationships and land/capex are potential moat mechanisms, to be tested through dated 10-Ks.
7. **MercadoLibre** (NASDAQ: MELI). Marketplace plus payments/logistics flywheel: test cash funding, credit loss provisions and fintech-specific risk rather than naive e-commerce gross margin. https://investor.mercadolibre.com/about-meli

These examples are **not** a precomputed empirical sample. Do not claim universal numerical thresholds, common metric averages, 'predictive accuracy' or 'backtested' success from these cases.

## Footnote: investor-style lenses (reading prompts, not criteria)

These names were removed from SKILL.md and the README in v1.1. They are listed only so readers know where some of the rubric's questions came from. No investor's actual method, thresholds or holdings-based inference is claimed, and nothing here is an endorsement by or of anyone named.

- Vijay Kedia SMILE: smaller firms + management + ambition + opportunity; interviews attribute this to him, but their true purchase-threshold settings are undisclosed.
- Ashish Kacholia: emerging smallcap business research and performance; holdings alone do not reveal original economics or execution prices.
- Mukul Agrawal: industry thesis, inflections and price/valuation confirmation; holdings snapshots subject to quarterly disclosure constraints.
- Sunil Singhania MEETS: management, earnings, events, timing and structural trends. Use individual interviews/firm publications to verify specific statements.
- Chris Mayer / 100 Baggers: high return on incremental capital and the capacity to reinvest for many years, often owner-oriented managers.
- Motilal Oswal / SQGLP: smaller starting size, quality, growth, longevity and a reasonable entry price.
- Saurabh Mukherjea: persistent cash-generative compounders and returns on capital over time.
- Porinju Veliyath / cyclical-value: undervalued improvement; normalized economics instead of peak P/E.
- Record investor-style suggestions as inferred *research modes*, never 'buy signals' proven by investor performance.

## Critical validation plan before use as automated investing strategy

Build a point-in-time universe of *all* eligible stocks, including delistings and fraud/liquidation cases where reconstructable; only use filings released by each formation date; ensure split-adjusted and dividend-adjusted total returns; verify historical universe/industry eligibility, spreads/liquidity, sector limits, fees/slippage and rebalancing; use walk-forward thresholds and fixed holdout periods. Evaluate precision/recall for 3x and 10x targets over 3, 5 and 7 years, false-positive/false-negative rates, drawdown, liquidity, sector exposure, price paid, rolling period stability and comparison to appropriate total-return indices (India smallcap/microcap and global smallcap as applicable). Winners-only retrospective analysis is inadequate. Where delisted names cannot be reconstructed, expressly state survivorship bias and do not imply rigorous validation.
